Sources & Method

HOW THIS MAP WAS MADE

Cabot Cove is fictional, and the series never published a map of it. Places come from fan research on the show and its Mendocino, California filming locations. Their positions on this model are interpretive: they keep the relationships the show describes (harbor below Main Street, the inn on the ridge, lighthouses on the headlands) without claiming a real layout. Reconstructed places fill in what a working Maine harbor town would need.

EPISODES AND MURDERS

Episodes use season and episode numbers in original CBS broadcast order (1984–1996). The murder map counts deaths in episodes set in Cabot Cove. Every murder gets a beam: solid where the place is recorded, faded where the place is estimated from the episode setup. Accidents, self-defense, past deaths and the two deaths recorded outside town are excluded. Faded beams mark places estimated from the episode setup rather than recorded. Death records come from fan-compiled research (v0.7, October 2026), are not verified against footage, and include some deaths that are not murders. Confidence is low to medium.

HOW THE NUMBERS WORK

Contemporary reporting from 1996 documented 64 murders in Cabot Cove over the show’s 12 seasons and cited a CBS population estimate of 3,560 residents (Deseret News).

(64 ÷ 12) ÷ 3,560 × 100,000 = 149.8

The U.S. averaged approximately 8.7 murders per 100,000 people annually across calendar years 1984–1996, calculated from historical FBI homicide rates (everycrsreport.com).

The comparison uses the years the series actually aired, rather than contrasting Cabot Cove with modern American crime statistics.

Caveat: Cabot Cove’s figure uses a fictional, essentially fixed population and a historical aggregate murder count that includes visitors. It is a well-sourced illustrative comparison, not a fully independently verified victim-by-victim census. The 64 reported murders also differ from the 59 placed on this map from fan research.
