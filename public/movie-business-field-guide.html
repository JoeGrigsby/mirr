Field Guide № 008

Sources & Method

This standalone page embeds 2,496 real film records from Movie Dataset 2001-2025.xlsx. Every dot represents a named film. Values are nominal, and the page works offline.

Which films are included?

  • For 2001–2024, 100 films per year are ranked by North American gross within an IMDb-popular extract. This is a sample, not an exhaustive top-100 theatrical chart.
  • For 2025, 96 entries from The Numbers’ released-in-year domestic chart were matched after catalog and episodic items were excluded. Selection by gross favors successful theatrical films and affects box-office relationships.
  • Gross figures are lifetime totals in their source snapshots, not year-of-release receipts. Release dates come from the source records and may not be North American opening dates.

What is measured?

  • 2,318 films have production budgets; 2,406 have opening-weekend grosses; 1,979 have both Rotten Tomatoes scores. Scores, dates and worldwide grosses reflect the different source snapshots recorded in the workbook.
  • Marketing budget, total budget, studio/distributor, parent company and film universe have no populated data. No values are estimated to fill those gaps. “Lead production company” is the first production company listed, not a verified distributor.
  • The color key prioritizes Animation, Sci-Fi and Horror tags, then uses the first broad genre in the source list. All genre tags are visible on the card. Oscar counts come from a title/year match to the Academy Awards extract; zero means no nomination was matched.

How to read the charts

  • Domestic means the North American theatrical market, not US-only. Worldwide is recorded worldwide theatrical gross. Box office is ticket revenue, not studio profit. Gross ÷ production budget is a ratio, not return on investment.
  • Pearson’s r and the least-squares trend use films with both selected values recorded. Dollar measures and positive counts use logarithms. Outlier “expected” values describe the fitted trend for the visible sample, not a forecast.
  • Current audience scores and later awards cannot explain earlier box-office results. The years and historical events shown are context, not causal evidence.

Data sources

  • Film list, budgets, grosses and IMDb ratings, 2001–2024 — Top 500–600 movies of each year, 1960–2024, compiled from IMDb by raedaddala on Kaggle (the “IMDb popular film extract”).
  • 2025 films and grosses — The Numbers, domestic box office for films released in 2025 (Nash Information Services).
  • Supplementary budgets and release details — TMDB movies, daily updates by alanvourch on Kaggle, drawn from The Movie Database (TMDB). This guide uses TMDB data but is not endorsed or certified by TMDB.
  • Rotten Tomatoes scores, 2001–2020 — Rotten Tomatoes movies and critic reviews dataset by stefanoleone992 on Kaggle.
  • Rotten Tomatoes scores, 2021–2024 — Dataset for Rotten Tomatoes movies, 1970–2024, shared on Reddit’s r/datasets.
  • Academy Award nominations and wins — The Oscar Award dataset by unanimad on Kaggle, from the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences’ awards records.
  • Film pages — each film card links to its source record and to its IMDb and Rotten Tomatoes pages.
  • Timeline context bands — Great Recession dates from the NBER U.S. business-cycle chronology; the COVID-19 pandemic as declared by the World Health Organization; the 2023 Writers Guild of America and SAG-AFTRA strikes.
  • Compiled workbook — Movie Dataset 2001-2025.xlsx, assembled for this guide from the sources above (research snapshot September 2026).
